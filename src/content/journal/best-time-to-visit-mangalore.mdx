Mangalore sits on the Karnataka coast, between the Arabian Sea and the foothills of the Western Ghats. That position gives the city a tropical climate with three very different seasons. None of them is a bad time to visit, but each one suits a different kind of trip.

Here is how the year breaks down, and what it means for a stay at Villa 19.

## October to February: the cool, dry season

This is the most comfortable time of year on the coast, and the busiest. The monsoon has cleared, the air is fresher, and evenings are pleasant enough to spend outdoors.

**Why it works:**

- Beach days are at their best. The sea is calmer and the sand at Panambur is clean and dry.
- Evenings on the lawn are comfortable, which matters when your group wants to eat, talk and linger outside.
- It is festival season. Mangalore Dasara usually falls in September or October, and the city is lit up for it. Kambala, the traditional buffalo race held in slushy paddy fields, runs through the winter months in villages around the region.

**What to know:** December and the New Year week book up early, for villas and flights alike. If your dates fall around Christmas, Diwali or the year end, plan well ahead.

## March to May: the hot season

Temperatures rise and humidity climbs, especially from April. Afternoons are best spent indoors or in the shade.

**Why it works:**

- School summer holidays make this the natural window for family reunions, when cousins across cities finally have the same free weeks.
- It is mango and jackfruit season. Local markets are full of fruit you will not find this fresh anywhere else.
- Beaches are lovely early in the morning and after sunset.

**What to know:** Plan outings for mornings and evenings. The villa's power backup means fans and air conditioning keep running even when the local supply does not.

## June to September: the monsoon

Mangalore receives some of the heaviest rainfall of any Indian city. The monsoon arrives in early June and the rain can be relentless through July and August.

**Why it works:**

- Everything turns green. The Western Ghats and the countryside around the city are at their most beautiful.
- It is the quietest time of year, so the city feels calmer.
- Rainy afternoons are well suited to what a private villa does best: long lunches, board games, and conversations that run into the evening.

**What to know:** Swimming in the sea is unsafe during the monsoon and beaches often fly red flags. Treat them as places to watch the waves from a safe distance. Allow extra time on the roads, and pack umbrellas and quick-dry clothing.

## So when should you come?

It depends on the trip you have in mind:

- **For beaches and sightseeing:** November to February.
- **For a school-holiday family reunion:** April and May, with outings planned early and late in the day.
- **For a quiet, rain-soaked retreat:** July and August.
- **For a festival backdrop:** late September and October, around Dasara.
